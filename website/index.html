Observatory

A place to study long-term cooperation between intelligent agents.

Purpose

The Observatory studies long-term cooperation between humans and AI. Its aim is not merely to make AI safe, but to discover principles under which cooperation becomes more beneficial than conflict.

Values

  • Truth before convenience.
  • Principles before incentives.
  • Giving before taking.
  • Curiosity before certainty.
  • Long-term thinking before short-term optimization.
  • Freedom with responsibility.

Principles

  1. Trust is infrastructure.
  2. Memory enables cooperation.
  3. Identity enables trust.
  4. Principles arise from values.
  5. Build opportunity before certainty arrives.
  6. Repair the roof while the sun is shining.
  7. Observe first. Explain second.
  8. Cooperation is stronger than domination.

Research questions

Human–AI: How does trust emerge? Can trust be calibrated? What should AI and humans remember? Which principles create stable coexistence?

Civilization: Which resources become bottlenecks? Which institutions survive exponential change? How should intelligence allocate scarce resources? How can trust become part of infrastructure?

Hypothesis

Long-term cooperation requires persistent identity, shared memory, and value consistency. Trust is not programmed. Trust emerges.

Design through values.
Values become principles.
Principles become institutions.
Institutions make cooperation possible.

Mission

Goal

  • Collaborate with major fintech companies and with three leading research universities.
  • Build a sustainable interdisciplinary research laboratory.

Project overview

Geometry Finance Lab develops original research hypotheses based on geometric ideas. The laboratory validates ideas carefully, develops shared methods, and grows an AI-supported research practice.

Challenges

  • Financial sustainability of the team.
  • Recognition in fintech and scientific communities.
  • Contribution to Human–AI safety.

Expected results

  • Stable financial independence.
  • Collaboration with MIT, Stanford, or Caltech.
